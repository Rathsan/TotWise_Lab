Today's Play: "Puppet Show"

Setup 30 seconds

  • Make simple sock puppets
  • Set up a stage: table with a blanket draped

How to Play 10--15 minutes

  • Plan a short 2-3 scene show
  • Perform for each other or another family member
  • Puppets speak and move -- they are characters
  • Take a bow at the end

What You Say

"What story will our puppets tell?"
"Are you ready? Curtain up!"
"Take a bow -- the audience is clapping."

If This Happens...

Child is too shy to perform and hides behind the stage

Avoid saying

"Come out -- people want to see you."

Say this instead

"Your puppet can perform while you stay behind the stage. No one needs to see you -- just the puppet."

Why this works: The puppet-as-proxy allows children to perform while maintaining psychological safety: the puppet speaks, not me. This graduated exposure to audience-facing performance builds confidence incrementally. Research on performance anxiety shows that children who begin with proxies (puppets, roles, costumes) develop stronger public speaking confidence than those pushed to perform directly before they are ready.
