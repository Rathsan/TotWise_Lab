Age 2--3 Milestone Sharing something precious -- not just any toy but the favourite one -- is a genuinely difficult emotional challenge. Practising with a trusted adult before facing it with peers reduces the distress of the real moment.
💎

Today's Play: "Your Turn with My Favourite"

Setup 30 seconds

  • Child's favourite toy
  • Explicitly acknowledge: this is your MOST special one

How to Play 10--15 minutes

  • Parent asks to borrow the favourite toy
  • Child lends it for a short, defined time (30 seconds)
  • Parent cares for it carefully -- demonstrates trustworthiness
  • Returns it on time: it comes back

What You Say

"This is your most special toy. Can I hold it for 30 seconds?"
"I will be very careful -- I know it is precious."
"Here it is back -- safe and sound, right on time."

If This Happens...

Child cannot do it and clutches the toy

Avoid saying

"You have to practise sharing even the favourite."

Say this instead

"That toy is too precious right now. OK. Let us practise with the SECOND most special one. That still counts."

Why this works: Starting sharing practice with high-value objects is counterproductive. The research principle is to build the sharing schema with low-stakes objects first and gradually increase value as the child builds trust in the return. The parent demonstrating careful handling and on-time return is critical -- it builds the trust infrastructure that makes all sharing emotionally sustainable.
