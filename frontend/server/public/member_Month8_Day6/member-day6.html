Today's Play: "Count on a Walk"

Setup 30 seconds

  • Take a walk indoors or outdoors
  • Pick a counting target

How to Play 10--15 minutes

  • Count: doors, trees, red things, cars, birds
  • Make it a challenge: can we find 10 dogs today?
  • Count together and remember the total
  • Record (verbally) how many you found

What You Say

"How many red cars can we count on this walk?"
"We found 6! Can we beat 6 next time?"
"Mathematics is hiding everywhere -- we just have to look."

If This Happens...

Child loses count and starts over repeatedly

Avoid saying

"Keep up -- you lost the count."

Say this instead

"We lost the count -- let us use fingers as a tracker. Each time we see one, I hold up a finger. Then we count the fingers at the end."

Why this works: Environmental counting builds what mathematics educators call the mathematical disposition -- an orientation toward noticing and quantifying the world. Children who habitually count things they encounter develop mathematical fluency not through instruction but through practice integrated into daily life. The finger-tracking strategy provides an external working memory support that reduces cognitive load.
